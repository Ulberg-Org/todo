**3. Your two ideas:**
- **Content first, then infrastructure:** #66 already does this, the lazy way. Everything inside the server or the cluster (roles, databases, Kubernetes objects, and from Phase 4 the apps) is deleted with them by Azure, so no separate step or ordering is needed. A separate "destroy content" stage would be more code for the same result.
- **Access not torn down with the environment:** agreed, that was the real weak point. The first, failed destroy deleted CI's own access to the cluster, and that's what blocked every PR.

The decision is where access should live. I recommend making the resource group `rg-dev` permanent, owned by the `access` stack along with every role assignment on it:
- people, agents and CI (the D88 roles), all granted on the group rather than on `aks-dev`;
- a new cluster inherits the access on creation, so `destroy` never touches it;
- dev still costs nothing when stopped, because an empty group is free;
- the fallback empties the group instead of deleting it.

The alternative is to grant the same roles on the whole subscription. That's simpler, but it also covers prod later, which is too broad.
