❓ **Q8** - **Can notification emails stay invisible to the sharer?** Sending only to existing users protects third parties. But if the API returns even slightly faster for unknown emails (no email to queue), the sharer learns who has an account, which breaks D22. To stay safe:
- the share request only writes the grant;
- a background job sends the email later, out of band, so the API response and its timing are identical either way.

➡️ Yes: an async outbox table plus a worker, and the existing no-enumeration timing tests cover it.

❓ **Q10** - **Does sharing with a registered user count toward a per-recipient limit too?** Without one, an attacker can make many free accounts and flood one registered user with share emails, even with per-sharer rate limits.

➡️ Yes:
- at most one notification per recipient per day, grouping everything shared that day into one "you have new shared todos" email;
- users can turn notifications off in settings.

❓ **Q11** - **Who owns a shared todo after its owner deletes their account?** You said shared todos survive. The plan says only the owner can delete a todo, and only `admin:share_creator` holders can change grants, so after deletion nobody could clean it up. Options:
- (a) it becomes orphaned: grantees keep their bits, nobody can delete it or change grants, and it disappears when the last grant is gone;
- (b) ownership passes to the grantee who has held `share` longest;
- (c) each grantee who had `read` gets their own copy, and the original is deleted.

➡️ (a). It's the Unix behavior (files stay after the user is deleted, owned by a bare UID), and nobody gains rights they weren't given. Grantees also get to drop their own access ("leave", D27, moved from future work into v1), so the orphan can be fully removed.
