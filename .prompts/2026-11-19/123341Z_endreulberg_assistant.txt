Round 1 covers the root decisions that other parts of the plan hang off.

❓ **Q1** - **Who is the showcase for, and what does "done" mean?** The plan never says who looks at it. That decides how much polish the frontend needs, whether `prod` has to be up when someone visits, and whether the write-up (blog post or talk) is a deliverable. For example:
- (a) future employers or clients reviewing the repos;
- (b) a talk or blog series about infrastructure-first agentic development;
- (c) your own learning, with public repos as a side effect.

➡️ (b), with (a) as a bonus. The repos plus `.prompts/` are the artifact, and a live demo only needs to run during demos.

---

❓ **Q2** - **Do you realistically expect a second human (developer or lead), and roughly when?** Several designs assume a team that doesn't exist yet:
- PIM approval by another lead;
- removing D34 ("second lead");
- GitHub teams;
- Claude for app developers;
- EMU (Enterprise Managed Users) as the upgrade path.

If it stays solo, some of those should be designed for one person plus agents rather than "until someone joins".

➡️ Solo for the foreseeable future. Design for "one human plus N agents" as the normal case, and keep the multi-person machinery only where it costs nothing.

---

❓ **Q3** - **Which MCP clients must work for app users at launch?** Claude.ai and ChatGPT connectors expect OAuth Dynamic Client Registration (or client metadata documents), which Entra External ID doesn't support. That's the Keycloak trigger in §8. Claude Code and Desktop with a pre-registered client work without it.
- (a) Pre-registered clients only (Claude Code, Desktop);
- (b) also Claude.ai and ChatGPT connectors, which brings Keycloak in by Phase 8.

➡️ (a) for v1, with (b) written into PLAN as the first post-v1 step. Keycloak is a lot of surface area for a showcase.

---

❓ **Q4** - **When a todo is shared with an email, how does that person find out?** The plan stores grants by email and activates them on first sign-in, but never notifies anyone. If we send email, that's egress, an email provider, an abuse vector (spam through shares), and it undermines the no-enumeration rule (§6.3).

➡️ No notifications in v1. The sharer tells the person out-of-band, and the grantee sees the todo after signing in. Notifications go to §13 as future work.

---

❓ **Q5** - **What happens when an app user deletes their account?** The plan doesn't cover it, but a public sign-up app with EU users needs an answer under GDPR. Open sub-points: their owned todos, the grants they made to others, and the grants others made to them.

➡️ Self-service "delete my account" in v1:
- their owned todos are deleted, along with every grant on them;
- grants they made on other people's todos stay, because there's no cascade (§6.2);
- grants to their email are removed;
- their telemetry already holds only hashed IDs.

This would be a new decision.

---

❓ **Q6** - **What happens when an app user changes the email on their account?** Ownership is by user ID, but grants are by email. After a change, the user loses every share made to the old address, and whoever signs up with the old address later gets them. That's the "pending shares" risk from §5.2 again, in a new form.

➡️ Grants bind to the user ID at the first verified sign-in, and are matched by email only while still pending. After that, an email change doesn't move them, and a new owner of the old address gets nothing.

---

❓ **Q7** - **Does the environment run all the time?** The plan estimates about USD 175–190 per month for `dev`, and about USD 350 with `prod` running 24/7. The plan lists stopping it between demos as a "savings lever", but never actually decides it.
- (a) both run 24/7;
- (b) `dev` is stopped outside working sessions and `prod` runs 24/7;
- (c) both are started on demand, by a CI workflow with your approval.

➡️ (b). `prod` is what visitors see. If the answer to Q1 is "demos only", then (c).
