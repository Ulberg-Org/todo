Yes, and that's one of the clearest wins. Everything in today's hand-written `local/envoy.yaml` comes from facts `platform.yaml` already declares:

| In `local/envoy.yaml` today | Comes from in `platform.yaml` |
|---|---|
| a cluster per service | `services:` |
| two endpoints (5001, 5002) | `replicas.min: 2` (the local overlay only says "start at port 5001") |
| routing `/` to the backend | `routes: [ /api, /mcp ]` per service (and `/` for the frontend later) |
| health check on `/health/ready` | `health.ready` |

So in the end:
- **locally**, the renderer writes Envoy's config, the instance count in `make up` and the compose services;
- **in the cluster**, the same renderer writes Envoy Gateway's routes (`HTTPRoute`) from the *same* `routes` and `health` fields.

Locally and in Azure it's then the same Envoy, with the same routes and the same health rule, from one source. Add a service, a route or a third replica in `platform.yaml`, and both places follow. Nobody edits Envoy by hand.

Until the renderer exists (Phase 2), today's `envoy.yaml` stays as it is. PR 2 is fine to merge as the working starting point, and the renderer replaces those files later.
