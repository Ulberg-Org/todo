Applying that rule right away to part 2. The proxy design is exactly the brittle kind:
- **Everything in the VM would have to use the proxy:** Docker, kind's node images, the pods themselves (Argo CD pulls charts, Grafana downloads plugins), NuGet, Nix, and Claude Code itself, whose Node `fetch` ignores proxy settings by default.
- **Failures would be hard to read:** anything that forgets the proxy fails in ways that look unrelated.
- **The pieces would be ours to maintain:** a profile of our own on top of `sandbox-exec`, which Apple has deprecated.

So I'm going with the simpler design Trail of Bits' `coop` uses for Lima. Your Mac stays protected:
- **No mounts:** the VM can't see your files, keys or logins.
- **Your Mac's own services are closed:** Lima's `hostLoopback: false` (one line, Lima 2.3) refuses the VM's connections to your Mac's `localhost`, so Postgres, Grafana and your cluster's API are out of reach.
- **Nothing of yours runs Claude's code:** its work only reaches you as a PR.

The VM may reach the internet freely. That's the risk we accept: a tricked Claude could send out what's in the VM, which is the app's GitHub token (1 hour, can't approve or merge), its Claude sign-in and the code. The code is public anyway. It could also reach devices on your LAN.
