Lima it is. Per the grill-before-building rule, one round first: switching to Lima opens new decisions. The biggest is in Q1: inside the VM Claude needs Docker, and Docker access means root in the VM. So a firewall *inside* the VM is only advisory, and the network limit has to sit outside it.

❓ **Q1 - Where the network limit sits**:
- In the VM Claude can do anything, including changing iptables through a privileged container (kind needs those). So egress must be enforced on your Mac.
- The VM gets its own network (Lima's `vzNAT`). macOS's firewall (`pf`) lets it reach only one place, a small proxy on your Mac. The proxy allows the host names on the D78 allowlist (it checks the host each HTTPS connection asks for) and blocks the rest.
- Claude can't touch either one. Setting up `pf` needs `sudo` on your Mac once.

➡️ Yes: `vzNAT` + `pf` + an allowlisting proxy on your Mac. Before building I'd research which proxy, under the supply-chain rule.

---

❓ **Q2 - The GitHub token**:
- Adding the token on the way out, as sbx does, means decrypting the traffic in the proxy (a CA certificate trusted by the VM). That's building our own sbx.
- Instead, your Mac mints the app's 1-hour token and copies it into the VM, renewing it while the VM runs. It can push branches and open PRs, never approve or merge.

➡️ Yes: the token inside the VM, and no decrypting proxy for now.

❓ **Q3 - The repo and the way out**:
- None of your folders are mounted.
- Claude clones the repo from GitHub inside the VM and runs `make up` there from its own clone. Its work leaves only as a PR (`claude-push`), which you review before anything of it runs on your Mac.

➡️ Yes.
